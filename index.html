Best AI for field service in 2026

By Arthur Allilaire, co-founder and CTO of Kenner · Vendor facts checked on 10 November 2026

Every field service suite now has AI in it, and a handful of specialists sit on top of them. Which is best depends on two things: the system your work orders already live in, and whether you need summaries and scheduling or an answer to the fault in front of the technician. This guide compares 12 tools on what their makers actually publish.

The short answer

If you run one of the big field service suites, its own AI is the obvious first step: Salesforce, Microsoft, ServiceNow, IFS, SAP, Oracle and ServiceMax all brief the technician and write the summary from the data already in the system. If the hard part is the fix itself, look at a specialist that answers from your own service history: Kenner, which builds a knowledge graph on top of whatever systems you run, for equipment service teams, Aquant for manufacturers' service organisations, Neuron7 for the very largest.

  • Equipment service teams that want answers from their own job history, on any system: Kenner
  • Service organisations of equipment manufacturers: Aquant
  • Enterprises with 1,000 or more technicians and complex equipment: Neuron7
  • Teams on Salesforce Field Service: Salesforce Agentforce for Field Service
  • Teams on Dynamics 365 Field Service: Microsoft Copilot in Dynamics 365 Field Service
  • Teams on ServiceNow: ServiceNow Field Service Management
  • Teams on IFS Cloud: IFS Cloud Field Service Management with IFS.ai
  • Teams on SAP: SAP Field Service and Asset Management with Joule
  • Teams on Oracle Fusion Field Service: Oracle Fusion Field Service
  • Asset-centric service teams on ServiceMax: PTC ServiceMax AI
  • In-house maintenance teams: MaintainX Assist
  • Trade contractors on Simpro: Simpro Lightning

What we compared

  • What it answers from. Only the records in one suite, or your manuals, past jobs and expert knowledge wherever they live. This decides whether it can help with the fault or only with the paperwork.
  • How a technician reaches it. Inside the field service mobile app, in a messenger, or on a phone call. A tool nobody opens on site helps nobody.
  • Whether it does the paperwork. Briefs before the job, summaries and reports after it, and whether they land in the system of record.
  • What you need first. Most suite AI needs that suite, and often an add-on licence on top.
  • Price. As published by the vendor. Most do not publish one.

Then look at where it stops: most suite AI summarises what is in the record, and if the answer your technician needs is in a manual, an old job or a colleague's head, a specialist that reads those will go further.

Our knowledge is in senior technicians' heads and old work orders

This is what the specialists are for. Kenner, Aquant and Neuron7 all build their answers from your own service history. Kenner and Aquant also capture what the technician says on the job, so the next person gets it.

Our technicians will not open another app

Look at how the tool reaches them. Kenner works in WhatsApp, iMessage, Teams, RCS or a phone call. Aquant works by voice call or SMS. Neuron7 and the suite assistants sit inside the system the technician already uses.

We mainly want less paperwork

Most tools here now draft the summary. Microsoft Copilot does it at no extra licence cost, Simpro Lightning writes the job up from the technician talking, and Kenner writes the report from a voice note and puts it back in your FSM, ERP or CRM.

MaintainX Assist is built for that and cites the manual or work order its answer came from. MaintainX publishes its prices and has a free plan, so check which plan includes Assist.

Questions and answers

What is the best AI for field service?

There is no single best one. For most companies the best first step is the AI in the field service suite they already run. For equipment service teams whose problem is the fix itself, a specialist that answers from their own job history, such as Kenner, Aquant or Neuron7, does more. This guide is published by Kenner, so weigh that.

What does AI actually do in field service?

Four things, mostly. It briefs the technician before the job from the history of the customer and the asset. It helps troubleshoot the fault. It writes the summary or report afterwards. And in the office it helps plan, schedule and dispatch.

Is the AI built into my field service software enough?

For briefs and summaries, often yes. For troubleshooting it depends on where your knowledge is. Suite AI answers from what is in the suite. If the fix is in a PDF manual, in a ten-year-old work order in another system or in someone's head, check whether the tool can read it.

How much does AI for field service cost?

Few vendors say. On the pages we checked, Salesforce lists Agentforce for Field Service at $125 per user per month on top of its Field Service licences, Microsoft includes Copilot in the $105 per user per month Dynamics 365 Field Service licence, and MaintainX has plans from free to $65 per user per month. Kenner, which we make, is free while in beta and has not published prices for afterwards. The rest quote on request.

Does field service AI work offline?

Several vendors say their mobile app works offline. None of the pages we checked says that the AI features themselves do. If your technicians work in basements and plant rooms, ask the vendor that exact question.

Will AI replace field service technicians?

No. Every tool here works by giving a technician information or taking paperwork off them. The repair, the safety decisions and the responsibility stay with the person on site.

How should we evaluate an AI tool for field service?

On your own jobs. Pick one team, connect the tool to your real work orders, and measure something you already track, such as first-time fix, callbacks or time spent on reports, before and after. A demo on the vendor's data tells you little.
