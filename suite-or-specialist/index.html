Best AI for Field Service

AI built into your FSM, or a specialist on top?

By Arthur Allilaire, co-founder and CTO of Kenner · Vendor facts checked on 10 November 2026

The short answer

Use the AI in your field service suite for what the suite already knows: briefs, summaries, record updates and scheduling. Add a specialist when the answer the technician needs is not in the suite, because it is in a manual, an old job or a colleague's memory. Most service organisations will end up with both.

  • Salesforce Agentforce for Field Service: suite AI with a brief, troubleshooting and a summary.
  • Microsoft Copilot in Dynamics 365 Field Service: suite AI included in the licence.
  • ServiceNow Field Service Management: suite AI with guidance, summaries and scheduling agents.
  • Kenner: a specialist that builds a knowledge graph of your units, jobs, alarms, parts and manuals. Our product.
  • Aquant: a specialist built from your manuals, service records and expert knowledge.
  • Neuron7: a specialist layer inside the suite, built from your case history.

What suite AI is good at

It is already there, it already has permission to see your records, and it writes back to them without an integration. For the brief before the job and the summary after it, that is most of what you need.

It is also where scheduling and dispatch AI lives. None of the specialists in this guide describes doing that.

Where it stops

Suite AI answers from the suite. Read the vendor's own description of what the technician guidance is based on: several of the pages we checked do not say, and two describe features that stop at summaries and record updates.

The specialists are built the other way round. They start from the manuals, the old work orders and what experienced technicians know, connect that to each asset, and answer from it. Kenner calls the result a knowledge graph. Neuron7 describes a graph of past cases. Aquant describes capturing expert knowledge.

A specialist is also not confined to the field service system. Kenner, which we make, connects to the CRM and ERP as well, so it can order the part and prepare the quote, and it reports what breaks across your units and which faults take longest to diagnose.

How to decide

Take ten recent jobs that went badly: callbacks, second visits, escalations. For each, ask where the information that would have prevented it was. If it was in the work order history in your suite, the suite's AI will find it. If it was somewhere else, that is the case for a specialist.

Questions and answers

Can we use both?

Yes, and many will. The specialists in this guide are built to sit on top of a field service system, not to replace it.

Is a specialist harder to roll out?

It needs a connection to your systems, which the suite's own AI does not. Kenner describes connecting in the first week and running a two-week pilot with five to ten technicians. Ask any specialist how long it takes before technicians get a useful answer.
