Best AI for field service technicians in 2026

By Arthur Allilaire, co-founder and CTO of Kenner · Vendor facts checked on 10 November 2026

The short answer

The best AI for a field service technician is one they will actually use on site: it answers from that company's own jobs and manuals, can be reached without stopping work, and takes the follow-up off them. Seven tools in this guide give troubleshooting guidance to the technician. They differ in what they read, how the technician talks to them, and what they do after the answer.

  • Kenner: a personal assistant by text or phone call that answers from the history of the unit, then orders the part and writes the report. Our product.
  • Aquant: voice and phone-camera guidance built from the company's manuals and service records.
  • Neuron7: step-by-step resolution guidance inside the CRM or field service system, for very large teams.
  • Salesforce Agentforce for Field Service: a troubleshooting dialogue in the Salesforce mobile app that can use photos, manuals and sensor data.
  • Oracle Fusion Field Service: AI-guided troubleshooting with handoff to a live operator.
  • PTC ServiceMax AI: a chat assistant over work orders, parts and installed products.
  • MaintainX Assist: manual and history search with sources cited, for in-house maintenance teams.

What makes AI useful to a technician

A technician does not need a summary of the work order. They need to know what this fault usually is on this model, what fixed it last time, and what to check first. That takes three sources: the manual, the history of the asset, and what colleagues have learned on similar jobs.

So the first question to ask of any tool is what it reads. An assistant that only sees the records in one system can tell you what was logged. One that also reads manuals and old jobs can tell you what to do.

How the technician reaches it matters as much

Half of field work is done with dirty hands, in bad light or on a ladder. Tools that take a phone call or a voice note get used there. Tools that need an app opened and a query typed get used in the van afterwards, if at all.

Kenner and Aquant both work by voice call and message without a new app, and they are the only two here that answer an ordinary phone call. The suite assistants live in the suite's mobile app, which is fine where technicians already work in it all day.

An answer, or the job done

After the answer, the technician still has to order the part, write the report and get the quote out. Ask which of those the tool does. Kenner, which we make, does all three from the same conversation, in the FSM, ERP or CRM the company already runs. Aquant documents the repair in the service record, and Oracle's start-of-day brief reserves parts. MaintainX Assist advises only.

What is not in this list

Microsoft Copilot in Dynamics 365 Field Service, SAP's Joule, IFS.ai, ServiceNow and Simpro Lightning all help technicians with briefs, summaries and record updates. On the pages we checked, their makers either do not describe equipment troubleshooting or do not say what the guidance is based on, so they are compared in the main guide and not here.

Questions and answers

Can a technician just use ChatGPT?

For general theory, yes. A general chatbot has not seen your work orders, your site notes or the service history of the asset, so it cannot say what fixed this unit last time, and it can be confidently wrong about a specific model. Check anything it says against the manual.

Do these tools replace calling the senior technician?

They cut down the routine calls. The tools that learn from your own jobs are in effect a way of giving every technician what the senior one already knows. The unusual fault still gets a phone call.

Can a technician phone the AI?

Two of the tools here answer an ordinary phone call: Kenner and Aquant. The suite assistants from Salesforce, Microsoft and ServiceNow take speech, but inside their own mobile apps.
